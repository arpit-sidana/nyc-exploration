# Search Playbook

Read only the section relevant to the requested mode.

## Source layers

Use multiple layers because NYC events are fragmented across platforms.

1. **Official sources:** organizer, venue, artist, restaurant, museum, park, university, accelerator, and conference calendars.
2. **Ticket and event platforms:** Eventbrite, Luma, Partiful, DICE, Resident Advisor, Meetup, Bandsintown, Songkick, Spotify Events, and venue ticketing partners.
3. **Public discovery:** NYC Parks, NYC.gov Events, Brooklyn Bridge Park, Downtown Alliance, DoNYC, Time Out New York, The Skint, Gothamist, New York Magazine, BRIC, and neighborhood publications.
4. **Social discovery:** public creator posts, organizer accounts, venue accounts, newsletters, and link-in-bio pages. Treat these as leads until verified.
5. **Authorized personal signals:** saved posts, bookmarked events, map lists, followed artists, and RSVP history. Use only when access is authorized and never store credentials or raw private exports.

For each candidate, retain the direct URL and record which source supplied the final verification.

## Weekend brief

- Cover each requested day separately.
- Group by borough or geography so travel friction is visible.
- Keep recommendations independent unless the user asks for an itinerary.
- Include a mix of ticketed anchors, lower-cost options, and weather-safe alternatives.
- Avoid repeating the same venue or generic format across days unless the programming materially changes.
- If a requested quota exceeds the number of credible matches, label weaker inventory rather than disguising it as high confidence.

## Neighborhood exploration

Build a compact, walkable route rather than a citywide list. Combine:

- two to four food or drink stops with distinct signature items;
- a main commercial or cultural corridor;
- architecture, murals, waterfront, park, or public-space interest;
- one unusual shop, market, gallery, or local business;
- an optional low-friction movement activity;
- hours, approximate spend, transit logic, and a one-line weather assessment.

Offer alternatives instead of prescribing an exhausting morning-to-night itinerary.

## Professional opportunities

Search roughly two to eight weeks ahead across official company and community calendars, Luma, Eventbrite, Meetup, universities, accelerators, venture firms, and conference sites.

Prefer:

- product demonstrations and showcases;
- named speakers with relevant operating experience;
- focused panels, fireside chats, and substantive talks;
- credible conferences with a clear value case;
- after-work weekday timing when the user has a standard work schedule;
- transparent pricing or meaningful free/discounted access.

Down-rank:

- generic networking with no program;
- vague founder or AI happy hours;
- events built primarily for lead generation;
- expensive conferences without strong speaker or access value;
- hands-on workshops, hackathons, and buildathons unless requested;
- full workday events that conflict with the user's availability, unless exceptional.

Include organizer, speakers, format, registration status, exact timing, location, price, and why the opportunity is professionally relevant.

## Food and coffee validation

Verify the current Google rating and review count on the day of research. Popularity alone is not enough; rank using the combination of:

- cuisine or concept fit;
- a specific dish, preparation, bean, pastry, or service format;
- review quality and meaningful review volume;
- current hours and reservation difficulty;
- price and value;
- novelty relative to known history;
- proximity to the requested route or event.

Do not recommend a vague category such as “try a coffee crawl” or “find a unique restaurant.” Convert it into named, verified stops or omit it.

## Confidence labels

- **Confirmed:** current official source verifies the actionable details.
- **Recurring/date-dependent:** the format is established but the requested occurrence still needs confirmation.
- **Needs final check:** one or more material details remain unresolved.

State what is unresolved instead of using confidence language as decoration.
